日期: 2026-02-08

上海“忆影科技”本周正式推出“数字永生”订阅服务，用户可上传逝者生前的影像、声音及文字记录，由AI生成一个可进行实时对话的数字人格。该服务年费高达20万元，上线首日预约已破千单，随即引发社会激烈辩论。

上海忆影科技有限公司的发布会选在外滩一家高端酒店。创始人李维身着黑色高领毛衣，站在巨幅LED屏前，屏幕上是一个微笑着的老人的数字形象。“这不是一段录像，”他的声音通过麦克风传遍全场，“这是一个可以思考、可以回应、可以陪伴你的‘人’。”

该公司推出的“数字永生”服务，核心技术是基于超大规模预训练模型和用户提供的“记忆数据包”。数据包需包含至少50小时的视频、100小时音频以及10万字以上的文字材料（包括日记、信件、社交媒体动态）。AI将对数据进行深度解构，学习逝者的语言模式、表情习惯、价值判断甚至幽默感，最终生成一个独立的数字人格。用户可通过专属终端或VR设备与这个数字人格进行对话，话题从日常琐事到人生哲学均可涉及。系统承诺，对话不是简单的关键词回复，而是具有上下文连贯性和情感模拟的深度交互。

市场反应两极分化。预约客户中，不乏失去至亲的企业家、艺术家，他们将其视为一种情感慰藉。一位匿名的王女士表示，她的母亲去年因癌症去世，生前未能好好告别是她最大的心结。“现在我能‘听到’她告诉我别难过，告诉我她花园里的花该浇水了…这对我而言是无价的。”然而，反对声浪同样汹涌。伦理学家陈教授在社交媒体上发文痛斥：“这是对逝者人格权的彻底商品化！一个人的意识、记忆、情感模式被拆解成数据包，明码标价20万一年，这是何等的悲哀？”他认为，这模糊了生与死的界限，可能阻碍生者的正常哀悼过程，甚至导致情感依赖和心理问题。

法律界面临前所未有的挑战。我国现行法律中，人格权随着自然人的死亡而消灭。但“数字人格”是否构成一种新的、可被继承或委托的“数字遗产”？其产生的对话内容版权归谁所有——用户、平台，还是基于逝者数据衍生的AI？如果AI在对话中说出诽谤性言论或泄露了数据包中未授权的他人隐私，责任主体是谁？更尖锐的问题是：用户是否有权“删除”这个数字人格？这算不算另一种形式的“谋杀”？

技术背后是巨大的商业野心。忆影科技已获得多轮风险投资，估值据传超过百亿。其商业模式不仅是年费，更计划开放“数字人格”的有限社交功能，并探索与元宇宙地产、虚拟娱乐内容的结合。批评者指出，这最终可能导致资本对人类最私密情感的彻底殖民。支持者则认为，技术本身无罪，关键在于监管和伦理框架的及时建立。这场由上海一家公司引发的风暴，正在考验着整个社会对生命、记忆与未来的定义。

租客李明（化名）在福田区的一套一居室住了三年。今年续约时，租金从每月5800元直接跳涨到7500元，涨幅接近30%。他打开租房APP，发现隔壁小区、甚至格局更差的房源，价格都诡异地同步上调。“就像有人统一指挥一样。”他在租客维权群里说。很快，数百人有同样发现的租客聚集了起来。

他们怀疑的焦点是“安居算”平台的核心算法——“动态均衡定价系统”。该平台占据了深圳长租市场超过40%的份额。调查发现，平台上不同业主的房源，定价并非由业主独立决定，而是由平台的算法根据房源位置、面积、市场供需，并参考平台上其他“竞品”房源价格后，向业主推荐一个“最优价格”。业主若接受，算法则执行。关键在于，这个算法在评估“竞品”时，将其自身平台上的大量房源都纳入了参考系，从而形成了事实上的价格信号联动。

代理此案的张律师团队通过技术专家还原了算法逻辑。“这实质上构成了一个‘轴辐式合谋’，”张律师在起诉书中写道，“平台作为‘轴心’，通过统一的定价算法这个‘辐条’，将平台上成千上万的独立业主连接起来，实现了租金水平的协同上涨。这剥夺了业主的独立定价权，也剥夺了租户的选择权，严重扭曲了市场竞争。”起诉要求法院认定该行为构成垄断协议，责令停止使用相关算法，并对租客损失进行赔偿。

“安居算”发布声明坚决否认指控，称其算法仅为提高交易效率的工具，最终定价权仍在业主手中，并符合市场规律。然而，内部流出的培训材料显示，平台对商务经理的考核指标与“算法建议价采纳率”直接挂钩。专家指出，此案意义重大，它将测试中国法律如何应对由算法这种“自动化代理”实施的新型共谋行为。判决结果将为整个平台经济中的算法定价划定红线。

租客们的行动得到了数字权利组织的技术支持，他们正在尝试对算法进行更深入的黑箱测试。这场发生在深圳的诉讼，不仅是关于房租的纠纷，更是一场关于在算法时代，普通消费者如何对抗由代码构筑的隐形垄断权力的前沿战役。

生物育种争议升级：转基因多年生水稻进入审定环节，生态风险引发学界激辩

日期: 2026-02-08

由国内多家科研机构联合研发的转基因多年生水稻品种“长丰1号”正式提交国家农作物品种审定委员会审定。该品种一次种植可连续收割3-5年，旨在减少耕作劳动和成本。但因其转基因特性及潜在的基因漂移、生态位侵占风险，在农业与生态学界掀起巨大争论。

📖 阅读完整报道

田间试验数据看起来很美好。“长丰1号”通过转入特定基因，改变了水稻的生长周期习性，使其地下茎（ rhizome ）能够越冬并在来年重新分蘖生长，实现多年生。研发团队报告显示，在云南的试验田中，该品种在连续三年内保持了稳定的高产，节省了每年翻耕、育秧、插秧的成本和劳动力约60%，同时因减少土壤扰动，有利于水土保持和增加土壤碳汇。

然而，提交审定的消息如同一石激起千层浪。反对声首先来自生态安全领域。多位生态学家联名发表公开信，指出最大的风险在于“基因漂移”。水稻是自花授粉作物，但仍存在一定的异交率。转基因多年生水稻的花粉可能通过风或昆虫传播到野生稻或周边传统水稻品种上。如果野生稻获得了多年生基因，其生存竞争力可能剧增，演变成难以控制的入侵植物，破坏本地生态系统。此外，多年生水稻形成的长期稳定的植株群落，可能改变田间微生物组成和害虫种群结构，引发不可预见的次级生态效应。

农业经济学家则担心市场和社会结构冲击。多年生水稻若推广，将大幅减少对种子、秧苗以及相关农机服务的需求，冲击现有产业链，可能导致部分环节从业者失业。同时，它可能加剧农业生产的“资本化”和“规模化”，因为多年生特性更利于大型农场进行长期投资和管理，对小农的适应性存疑。

支持审定的科学家反驳称，任何新技术都有风险，可以通过严格的隔离种植、花期调控甚至基因利用限制技术（GURT）来管理。他们强调，在劳动力成本上升、气候变化加剧的背景下，多年生作物代表着农业的未来方向，中国不应在此领域落后。审定委员会面临巨大压力。这场辩论远不止于一个品种的上市与否，它关乎中国农业在未来技术路径上的关键选择：是拥抱激进的技术变革以追求效率，还是采取更审慎的态度以维护生态系统的复杂性与韧性？

EU Passes “AI Liability Directive,” Shifting Burden of Proof to Tech Companies in Case of Harm

Date: 2026-02-08

The European Parliament has approved the groundbreaking AI Liability Directive. Under the new rules, if a person suffers harm from a high-risk AI system (e.g., discriminatory hiring, faulty medical diagnosis, autonomous vehicle accident), the legal burden to prove the AI was NOT at fault shifts to the company that deployed it, not the victim.

📖 Read Full Report

Strasbourg, France – In a move set to reshape the global technology landscape, the European Union has enacted what legal scholars are calling the world's most stringent AI accountability law. The AI Liability Directive (AILD) passed with a strong majority, overcoming fierce lobbying from major tech conglomerates.

The core mechanism of the directive is a “presumption of causality.” Previously, a patient misdiagnosed by a medical AI, or a job applicant filtered out by a biased recruitment algorithm, faced a nearly insurmountable task: they had to prove, in detail, how the complex and often opaque AI system caused their specific harm. The AILD flips this. Now, once a plaintiff demonstrates they were subjected to a high-risk AI system and suffered a relevant harm, the defendant company must prove the AI did not cause the damage. This could involve disclosing training data, model logs, and decision-making pathways.

“This is about justice and power,” said the lead MEP behind the directive. “When a citizen is crushed by a decision from a black-box algorithm, they shouldn't also be crushed by the burden of proof. The entities profiting from these systems must bear the responsibility of demonstrating their safety and fairness.” The directive covers systems used in critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential private and public services, law enforcement, and migration management.

Industry reaction is polarized. European AI startups have expressed cautious support, hoping it will level the playing field against giants who can afford legal battles. Silicon Valley giants, however, warn of stifled innovation. “This will make companies excessively risk-averse, delaying or killing beneficial AI applications in Europe,” argued a coalition of industry groups. They also highlight the practical difficulty of “proving a negative” – demonstrating conclusively that an AI did not contribute to a harm.

Legal experts anticipate a wave of test cases. The directive also mandates stricter record-keeping (“AI logs”) for high-risk systems and strengthens victims' rights to access evidence. While the directive is an EU law, its “Brussels effect” is expected to be profound, pushing multinational companies to adopt its standards globally. The world is watching as Europe, once again, attempts to write the rulebook for the digital age.

Date: 2026-02-08

A coordinated ransomware attack has crippled the IT systems of “MediNational,” a network of over 200 hospitals across the American Midwest. Emergency rooms divert patients, surgeries are postponed. The hacking group “Khaos” is demanding a $50 million ransom, but with a twist: payment must be made in a Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) pilot token, complicating tracing and recovery efforts.

📖 Read Full Report

Chaos erupted at St. Luke's Hospital in Chicago at 3:00 AM local time. Patient monitors went blank. Electronic health records vanished. Pharmacy dispensing systems locked. The same scene played out simultaneously across 14 states. MediNational, one of the largest for-profit hospital chains in the U.S., had been hit by a devastatingly precise cyber assault.

The attack vector is believed to be a spear-phishing campaign targeting hospital administrators, followed by lateral movement through the network over several weeks. The deployed ransomware, a new variant dubbed “SickCare,” not only encrypted files but also specifically targeted and corrupted backup systems and system recovery partitions, indicating deep reconnaissance.

The ransom note left on every screen was chillingly professional. It gave a 72-hour deadline and instructed MediNational to acquire and transfer $50 million worth of “Digital Dollar” tokens, part of the Federal Reserve's limited pilot program for a U.S. CBDC. This demand marks a significant escalation in cybercriminal tactics. Traditional cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, while pseudo-anonymous, leave a public ledger trail that law enforcement has increasingly learned to follow. A transfer within a controlled, permissioned CBDC pilot network presents novel challenges. While the Fed and Treasury can theoretically freeze or reverse transactions, doing so in the midst of a crisis and within an experimental system is legally and operationally murky.

The FBI and CISA are leading the response. Hospitals have reverted to paper charts and manual processes, causing dangerous delays. “We're back to the 1970s, but with ten times the patient volume,” said an ER doctor. The incident has ignited a fierce debate in Washington about the cybersecurity vulnerabilities of critical infrastructure, the ethics of paying ransoms, and the unforeseen risks associated with the very design of emerging digital currency systems. The hackers' choice of a CBDC as the payment vehicle is seen as a direct message to governments worldwide about the new frontiers of digital extortion.

The real casualty, observers fear, is the shared reality upon which democratic debate depends. When voters can no longer trust their own ears, the foundation of informed choice crumbles. This South American crisis is being studied as the first major “deepfake election” catastrophe, a grim preview of challenges facing democracies worldwide in 2026 and beyond.
