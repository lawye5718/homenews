2026年2月25日，中国人民银行正式宣布，其酝酿多年的数字人民币跨境支付系统“星链”于今日零时全球上线。该系统首批接入了包括俄罗斯、伊朗、沙特阿拉伯、巴基斯坦、阿根廷在内的17个国家，覆盖了超过30%的全球大宗商品贸易流量。与传统的SWIFT系统不同，“星链”基于区块链技术，实现了跨境支付的实时清算与结算，平均交易确认时间从SWIFT的2-3天缩短至7秒。中国人民银行行长在新闻发布会上强调，“星链”旨在为全球贸易提供“更安全、高效、中立的支付选择”，并否认该系统具有任何地缘政治排他性。

然而，西方金融分析机构迅速发出警告。摩根大通的一份内部备忘录指出，“星链”的底层架构允许中国人民银行实时追踪每一笔跨境资金流动，这赋予了北京前所未有的全球资本监控能力。更关键的是，系统内置的“智能合约”功能理论上可以在特定条件下自动冻结或转移资产，这被一些法律学者视为“超主权金融权力”的体现。首批接入国名单极具象征意义：俄罗斯和伊朗长期受美国金融制裁，沙特阿拉伯是石油美元体系的基石，阿根廷则深陷美元债务危机。这些国家的共同选择，反映了全球南方对降低美元依赖的迫切需求。

市场反应立竿见影。消息公布后，离岸人民币对美元汇率一度飙升1.8%。黄金价格小幅上涨，被视为对潜在金融体系动荡的避险反应。美国财政部发言人称正在“评估‘星链’系统的技术细节及其对全球金融稳定的影响”，但未宣布任何具体反制措施。欧盟委员会则表示，将加速数字欧元的研发进程。分析认为，“星链”的上线并非要立即取代美元，而是构建一个平行的、由中国主导的支付轨道。它为受制裁国家提供了逃生舱，也为所有不满于美国金融霸权的国家提供了备选项。这场货币的“软战争”，刚刚进入新的、技术驱动的阶段。未来的冲突可能不再发生在航母战斗群之间，而是在区块链的共识算法和中央银行的数字账本里。

杭州市西湖区蒋村街道办事处的公示栏里，出现了一个新名字：“蒋村街道民生事项AI决策辅助专员（系统）”。这个没有实体、却拥有独立工号和审批权限的“AI公务员”，负责处理从低保申请复核到小型工程拨款初审等47项事务。根据试点方案，该系统由阿里巴巴云与浙江大学联合实验室提供技术支持，通过分析申请人的多维数据（包括社保记录、消费行为、甚至社交媒体情绪分析）来生成“建议批准”或“建议驳回”的初步结论，人类官员在此基础上做最终裁定。政府宣传材料称，该系统将审批效率提升了300%，并消除了“人情因素”和“主观判断不一致性”。

更深层的争议在于价值观编码。系统训练数据是中国过去十年的行政案例，这必然将历史上的某些潜规则或结构性偏见固化、放大。例如，对于来自特定地域或从事非正规职业的申请人，系统是否会不自觉地赋予更高的“风险权重”？支持者认为，AI至少是“一致地不公平”，而人类是“随机地不公平”。反对者则警告，这是用技术理性包装的新型权力垄断。蒋村的实验只是一个开始。据信，北京、上海、深圳等至少十个大城市正在筹备类似的“智慧政务”升级。一场关于谁在统治我们——是法律，是官僚，还是算法——的辩论，正在从学术期刊走向街头巷尾。

关键要点： 中国东北几个“收缩型城市”因房价极低、生活成本骤降，意外创造了适宜生育的经济环境，挑战了高收入必然导致高生育率的传统城市化理论。

最新公布的2025年人口数据显示，黑龙江省鹤岗市、辽宁省阜新市、吉林省辽源市的出生率出现了自1990年代以来的首次正增长，分别达到1.2%、1.05%和0.98%。这一现象与全国生育率持续下滑的趋势背道而驰，被学者称为“鹤岗悖论”。这些城市共同的特征是：经历了资源枯竭或产业衰退，人口大量外流（鹤岗过去十年流失了约25%的人口），导致房价暴跌至“白菜价”（一套两居室公寓均价在5-10万元人民币），生活成本仅为一线城市的五分之一到三分之一。

社会学者的田野调查揭示了反弹背后的微观逻辑。当住房、教育、医疗这“三座大山”的压力被极低的房价和宽松的竞争环境大幅缓解时，年轻夫妇的生育决策发生了根本变化。在鹤岗，许多从事远程工作的程序员、设计师、自媒体从业者（被称为“数字游民”）从北上广深迁入，用卖掉一线城市一个卫生间的钱，在这里全款买下大房子。他们发现，养育一个孩子的直接经济成本和机会成本（尤其是母亲的事业中断成本）远低于大城市。“在这里，孩子上学没有天价学区房，周末就是去河边玩，压力小了很多。”一位从杭州移居鹤岗的三十岁母亲说。她的家庭年收入约20万元，在鹤岗属于高收入阶层，生活品质远超在杭州时。

这一现象挑战了经典的人口转型理论。该理论认为，随着工业化和城市化，生育率会不可逆转地下降。但“鹤岗悖论”暗示，也许不是城市化本身，而是特定阶段城市化伴随的极端竞争、高生活成本和空间挤压抑制了生育。当城市“收缩”到一定程度，形成一种低压力、高宜居性的新平衡时，生育意愿可能回升。这为中国乃至全球应对低生育率危机提供了一个意想不到的、非主流的思路：与其拼命将资源注入大城市催生内卷，不如主动培育一批“宜居的收缩城市”，让人们能够“负担得起生活，从而负担得起孩子”。当然，这涉及到深层的资源分配和区域发展政策转向。鹤岗的春天，或许只是特例，但它的确在人口学的寒冬里，投下了一缕异样的光。

一位化名“李默”的外卖平台算法优化员向北京朝阳区劳动人事争议仲裁委员会提起仲裁，要求确认其与某头部外卖平台存在事实劳动关系，并支付未休年假工资、加班费及社会保险。此案被称为中国“幽灵工作”维权第一案。李默的工作内容是：每天通过平台派发的任务，对配送路径规划AI的推荐结果进行“优化”和“纠错”，例如判断“AI建议的这条小路晚上没有路灯，应改为大路”，或“该小区西门已封闭，需改为东门”。他的工作直接影响了成千上万外卖骑手的路线和时效，是平台算法持续学习的“人工反馈”关键一环。然而，在法律上，他并非平台的员工，而是与一家位于江西的劳务外包公司签订的合作协议，按任务量计酬，没有底薪、没有社保、没有固定的工作时间和地点。

平台方的抗辩理由是：李默的工作具有高度灵活性和自主性，他可以选择接单与否，可以使用自己的设备在任何地点工作，其劳动成果是“优化建议”而非平台直接销售的产品，因此属于“民事合作关系”而非劳动关系。李默的代理律师则主张，平台通过算法对李默进行着事实上的管理：系统设定接单率、响应时间和质量标准，低于标准会扣减信用分乃至停止派单；工作内容、流程、规范完全由平台规定；李默的工作已深度嵌入平台核心业务运营，具有从属性和经济依赖性。此案的争议焦点在于：当管理权从人类主管转移到算法系统，当工作被拆解为离散的“任务”，传统的“人格从属性”、“经济从属性”、“组织从属性”三要素劳动认定标准是否仍然适用？

此案背后是数以百万计的“幽灵工人”。他们训练着AI，审核着内容，优化着算法，是数字经济的“基础设施”，却不在任何公司的花名册上。他们的劳动被隐形化，权益被悬置。案件的裁决将具有里程碑意义。如果李默胜诉，意味着大量被定义为“零工”的数字劳工可能被重新归类，平台企业的人力成本将急剧上升。如果平台胜诉，则意味着现有劳动法体系在数字时代出现了巨大的保护真空，一种没有雇主却受算法严格管控的新型劳动形态将被合法化。仲裁庭外，聚集了来自网约车、内容审核、数据标注等各行业的“幽灵工人”。他们举着的标语上写着：“算法在管理我，谁在对我负责？”

Key Takeaway: The EU launches a massive public cloud project to reduce reliance on US tech giants, framing it as a matter of digital sovereignty, data security, and economic resilience, which could fragment the global internet.

In a landmark move, the European Commission unveiled “EuropaCloud”, a continent-scale, publicly funded cloud infrastructure initiative. The project, budgeted at €45 billion over the next decade, aims to provide a secure, GDPR-native alternative to Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud for EU governments and critical industries (finance, health, energy). The announcement, made by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Brussels, was framed not just as an industrial policy, but as a “fundamental pillar of European strategic autonomy in the digital age.”

The technical blueprint mandates that all physical infrastructure (data centers, network hubs) must be located within EU borders, operated by a consortium of European companies (including Deutsche Telekom, Orange, and SAP), and subject to a new “European Data Custodianship” legal framework. This framework gives EU authorities unprecedented oversight and “golden key” access capabilities under strict judicial review, a direct response to fears over the US CLOUD Act, which compels US-based tech companies to hand over data regardless of where it is stored.

Reactions were sharply divided. French President Emmanuel Macron hailed it as “the moment Europe decided to own its digital destiny.” German Chancellor echoed the sentiment, emphasizing industrial data protection. Silicon Valley reacted with concern but not surprise. An AWS spokesperson stated they “remain committed to supporting European customers” while highlighting their existing €30+ billion investment in EU regions. However, analysts note that mandatory migration of sensitive public sector workloads to EuropaCloud would cut deeply into a lucrative market segment for the US giants.

The broader implication is the acceleration of “Splinternet” or “Digital Balkanization”. Following China's Great Firewall and Russia's sovereign internet, the EU is now institutionalizing a third major bloc of digital infrastructure defined by its own legal and values-based perimeter. For multinational corporations, this means increased complexity and cost: they may need to maintain separate data stacks for China, the EU, and the “rest” (still dominated by US clouds). EuropaCloud is more than a cloud project; it is a declaration of digital independence. It signals that the era of a globally uniform, US-led internet infrastructure is over. The future is fragmented, sovereign, and shaped by geopolitics as much as technology.

Key Takeaway: The no-code revolution faces a maturity crisis as users, having built critical business applications on proprietary platforms, now find themselves trapped, leading to a grassroots movement demanding open standards.

Tens of thousands of small business owners, freelancers, and “citizen developers” who rely on no-code/low-code platforms like Webflow, Bubble, and Adalo are staging a coordinated “build freeze” and social media protest. Their demand: enforceable data portability and application interoperability standards to prevent vendor lock-in. The protest, organized via Discord and Reddit communities, highlights a growing crisis in the democratized software movement. Users who built their entire online store, booking system, or internal workflow on these platforms now face exorbitant price hikes, unilateral changes to terms of service, and the terrifying prospect that their business logic and customer data are held hostage inside a walled garden they cannot escape.

“I built my boutique hotel’s entire reservation and management system on Bubble. It works great, but my monthly bill has tripled in two years. If I try to leave, I can’t take my database schema or the core workflows. I’d have to rebuild everything from scratch, which I can’t afford,” said Maria Rodriguez, a protester from Portugal. This sentiment is widespread. The protestors are not refusing to pay; they are refusing to start any new projects on these platforms until the companies commit to adopting an open-source export standard, similar to what Figma did with its design file format.

The platform companies have responded cautiously. Bubble issued a statement acknowledging “user concerns about long-term flexibility” and promised a “data export enhancement roadmap.” Webflow highlighted its existing CMS export features. However, analysts note that the core business model of many no-code platforms relies on this very lock-in—the “stickiness” of the platform ensures recurring revenue. Granting easy export would undermine that. The protest taps into a larger tension in the tech economy between the promise of democratization and the reality of platform power. It asks: who truly owns the digital tools and assets created in a democratized environment? The answer, currently, seems to be the platform provider. This “Silent Strike” may force a change, potentially prompting regulatory attention from bodies like the EU, which has already shown interest in platform fairness and data portability rules (e.g., under the Digital Markets Act). The no-code dream is hitting the hard wall of commercial reality.
